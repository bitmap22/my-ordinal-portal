Doge Block Manifest

Every transaction in a Dogecoin block, sorted and checked against the chain

Pick a block. The Doge Block Manifest reads the whole block and sorts every transaction into the same kinds as the shelves in Doge Cube's halls. The list is proved against the block's own header, and every transaction that carries something is read from its own bytes. Each comes with its fee and size, checked on two sites. It's free, for people and AI agents, and you can download it. The Bitcoin twin is the Block Manifest.

Try:

    Block

    Checked

    What's in it

    Transactions

    #TransactionFee (DOGE)Size (bytes)DOGE/kBKindsCarriesChecked

    Checked: ✓ agrees, ✗ differs, · nothing to check. The six marks are: in the list the header proves, its size, its fee, its kinds, its inscriptions, and read from its own bytes. Text in quotes like these was written by whoever made the transaction. It's shown as they wrote it, unchecked.

    For AI agents

    In this page: await window.agent.help(), then agent.read({height}), agent.items({kind, from, limit}), agent.item({n}), agent.checked() and agent.see(). Where the browser has WebMCP, the same commands are tools named dogemanifest_*. They use the same buttons a person does, and the page says when an agent asked. Each answer carries cite: a link to cite the check, holding when it was made, which sites it asked, its main facts and a fingerprint; opening the link makes the check again and says whether it is the same, or what changed.

    The bigger apps: Doge Cube, where you walk into any Dogecoin block in 3D (its kit's walkIn({height})), and its Bitcoin twin Agent Blockhall, with the Bitcoin Block Manifest. For any Doginal in a block, the Doge Provenance Check proves where it came from, read from the chain.

    Without a browser: the free Agent Blockhall MCP tool has the same Doge Block Manifest as its dogeBlockManifest tool. The block reader is one file, doge-manifest.js. More in llms.txt.

    Free, with no keys or accounts. It reads free public sites, gently: thedoginalsexplorer.com (blocks, transactions, inscriptions, dunes), BitPay's Bitcore (a second list of sizes and fees) and Blockchair (each transaction's own bytes, at most 30 a minute, as Blockchair asks).
