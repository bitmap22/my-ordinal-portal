5. Inscriptions and content made by others

The blockchain is public, and anyone can put anything on it. Inscriptions, names, messages, pictures and files shown on the portal belong to their own creators and owners. They are shown as they are found on the chain. We do not make them, check them or endorse them, and some may be offensive, misleading, unsafe or unlawful. For your safety, Agent Blockhall and Block Paint City never run an inscription's code in their own pages: a web page or script inscription is not opened there, and links to its own page on ordinals.com (with ordiscan.com as a back-up) instead. The one exception is Agent Blockhall's block feed, where a web page inscription, and a 3D model, plays in ordinals.com's own preview, inside a sealed frame that cannot reach our page, open windows or move you anywhere, and only while it is on your screen (not in Family mode). Its code runs on ordinals.com, not on our site, and what it shows is its creator's, not ours. If you open one anywhere else, you do so at your own risk.

18+ content and the Family Guard. To keep the portal family friendly, every picture and film from an inscription is checked in your own browser before it is shown clearly (in Agent Blockhall, its Play Together page and the Sentinel ID Viewer). While it is checked it is blurred. Moving pictures and films are checked on several frames. One that flashes more than 3 times a second (a seizure risk) is shown still, with a warning, until you choose to play it; and if your device is set to reduce motion, every moving picture is shown still until you play it. A picture your browser cannot open shows a note and a link to its own page instead. If the checker thinks it may be adult content, it stays hidden behind an 18+ warning: only show it if you are 18 or over. Words in text inscriptions, player names and chat have rude words starred out. Family mode (the shield button, top right in Agent Blockhall) hides every picture from an inscription until you choose. The checkers are free tools (NSFWJS and AllProfanity) and are not perfect: some adult or offensive content may still get through, and some harmless pictures may be hidden. Links to other sites, such as ordinals.com, open pages we do not check. Nothing is sent anywhere: every check happens on your own device.

The Safety Scan. About every hour, GitHub's computers look at and read every inscription of the latest Bitcoin blocks, without running any of it, and mark what to watch out for with a colour and a symbol: may be adult, may flash (a seizure risk), may be a scam, agent traps (words or code that try to give AI agents orders), risky code, miners or phone-killers, hate words and personal details. SVG drawings are drawn and looked at like pictures, and the words written inside sound files (and their cover pictures) are read. The marks show on the Safety Scan pages and over the parcels in Agent Blockhall. Every mark is a "may be": the scan uses free tools and simple rules, so it can mark harmless things and miss harmful ones, and no mark does not mean a file is safe. It is a warning to help you, not a judgement about anyone, and not advice. The pages never show or run an inscription; phone numbers, emails and hate words are masked so the scan never spreads them further. If you think something is marked wrongly, or a mark is missing, tell us.

If you believe something shown here infringes your rights, please tell us through Report a problem.

3D models

  • Draco by Google (Apache License 2.0), version 1.5.7: unpacks squeezed 3D model inscriptions so Agent Blockhall can draw them. It is loaded only when such a model comes, and only unpacks numbers: nothing from the inscription runs. Its licence is kept with it, in vendor/draco.

Models are drawn as their makers set them up in glTF 2.0 (by the Khronos Group): colour, metal and roughness, surface bumps, crease shadows, glow, glass, varnish and velvet, in a studio light with a soft shadow. The drawing code is our own, written for the portal, using these published methods:

  • Khronos PBR Neutral by the Khronos Group (Apache License 2.0): the colour curve that keeps a model's colours true.
  • Physically Based Shading on Mobile by Brian Karis (Epic Games): a short formula for how shiny surfaces reflect light.
  • Followup: Normal Mapping Without Precomputed Tangents by Christian Schüler: how surface bumps are found from a model's own picture placing.

Open standards for AI agents

The portal's agent kits, skill and MCP tool are our own, written for the portal. They follow these open standards, so any agent can use them:

  • Agent Skills (agentskills.io): the format of the Ord Portal skill.
  • Agent Skills Discovery via Well-Known URIs, a draft led by Cloudflare (version 0.2.0): where the skill is listed, so agents find it by themselves.
  • WebMCP, a draft of the W3C Web Machine Learning Community Group: how each page hands its kit to the browser's AI.
  • The Model Context Protocol (version 2026-07-28 and the older versions), with its extensions MCP Apps (the view in the chat), Tasks (long checks) and Skills over MCP (the skill): how the Agent Blockhall MCP tool talks to AI apps.

Following a standard is not endorsement: none of these groups made the portal or endorse it.
