Collection Check

Which pieces are a parent's real children, proven on the chain

Give a collection's parent inscription. The Collection Check reads every child's own transaction to see that it names the parent, and follows the parent's every move back to where it was made, to see that the parent was spent to make each child. That's ord's rule for a real child, so every piece it proves really belongs. It's free, for people and AI agents. Then walk into the collection's gallery room in Agent Blockhall, or check any one piece with the Provenance Check. The Dogecoin twin is the Doge Collection Check.

        For AI agents

        In this page: await window.agent.help(), then agent.check({parent}), agent.children({filter, from, limit}), agent.child({id}), agent.list({ids}) and agent.see(). Where the browser has WebMCP, the same commands are tools named collection_*. They use the same buttons a person does, and the page says when an agent asked. Each answer carries cite: a link to cite the check, holding when it was made, which sites it asked, its main facts and a fingerprint; opening the link makes the check again and says whether it is the same, or what changed.

        The bigger app is Agent Blockhall: a parent with children has a gallery room there. The Provenance Check proves where any one piece came from, and Watch tells the moment a parent moves (a new child is made by spending it).

        Without a browser: the free Agent Blockhall MCP tool has the same check as its collectionCheck tool. The reader is one file, collection.js, shared by this page and the tool. More in llms.txt.

        Free, with no keys or accounts. It reads free public sites: ordinals.com (and its back-ups) and mempool.space (and its copies), gently: at most 30 asks at once, then 6 a second per site. Inscriptions belong to their creators: this page links to them and doesn't show them. The Goosinals example's IDs come from Ordinals Wallet's collection list.
